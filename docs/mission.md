# The Mission: a complete, working world

> **We are not building an engine. We are building a working world - a complete, fully-functioning
> game.** We do not stop until the whole house is built and the mission is done and over, so the
> owner can finally play the *whole* game - and revise it while beta-testing the finished product.

This is the keel-level destination. Everything below the waterline - the tick, the parts, the gates,
the ethos of [optimization](optimization_ethos.md) and the [human keel](human_keel_doctrine.md) -
serves it. Those disciplines say *how* we build well. This says *what* we are building toward: not a
demo, not a showcase of engine features, not a pile of tested parts, but a **finished, playable
world**.

## What "complete" means

A player can start at the beginning, play to the end, and find a whole game the entire way - not a
tech demo with gaps papered over. Concretely, the house is complete when:

- **The world is full, not thin.** Enough hand-authored places, people, quests, encounters, and
  items that the world feels lived-in from the first room to the last, not stretched over procedural
  filler.
- **The systems are whole and connected.** Combat, jobs, progression, economy, factions, dialogue,
  and quests are not just present but *finished* and woven together - each reinforces the others.
- **The arc pays off.** A real beginning (onboarding that teaches without a manual), a real middle
  (branching choices that matter), and a real ending (an endgame worth the climb).
- **It is balanced.** The difficulty and reward curves hold across the whole span, start to summit,
  so the climb is fair and earned rather than a wall or a walkover.
- **It is polished.** The text, the pacing, the feedback, and the moment-to-moment feel are shaped,
  not merely functional.

## How we work toward it

- **Build toward the finished world, not isolated cleverness.** Before a slice, ask: *does the whole
  game need this to be complete?* Content volume, systems completeness, and polish are the work -
  not one more clever mechanic in a vacuum.
- **Do not stop at engine or feature boundaries.** A tested part is a plank, not the house. Keep
  building until the whole game is playable end to end.
- **Prove every plank, as always.** The gates do not relax because we are moving fast: `make check`
  green, evidence for every claim, honest labels. A finished world is one that *works*, and we prove
  that it works the same way we prove everything - the tests, the gates, the green CI.
- **Finish, then hand over.** The owner beta-tests the *whole* product once it is built. The job of
  the build is to reach completeness and deliver a whole game to revise - not to ship gaps and hope
  they are found in play.

The mission ends when there is genuinely nothing left to build for a complete game - and not before.
