# Faction Encyclopedia — the Powers of Aethryn

*No faction is the villain. The world's living conflict is the unresolved argument over the one thing
that matters — **who controls Forging, and how much they trust others with it** — and every faction is
a good-faith answer that becomes monstrous at its edges. Factions grant reputation, quests, access,
standing conflicts, and unique rewards; any can carry a full expansion. Each entry below carries its
history, philosophy, leadership, hierarchy, territory, strength, allies/enemies, internal fault-line,
recruitment, and what it gives a player who rises in it.*

Reputation is tracked per-faction on a standing ladder (Hostile → Wary → Neutral → Trusted → Sworn →
Inner). Rising with one often costs standing with its rival — the world remembers whose side you took.

## THE THREE GREAT POWERS (the world's argument)

### The Warden Concord — *Guard the craft* (control)
- **History.** Born in the terror after the Unforging; the survivors who took an oath to guard the
  Gates and Seeds so no half-taught Forger could Unforge the world again. They built the Wardenmarch
  and outlasted the Long Cinder by turning knowledge into something you must be *trusted* with.
- **Philosophy.** *Authorization before capability.* The Unforging proves the craft must be licensed,
  tested, and rank-gated. Power un-earned is the world's death.
- **Leadership & hierarchy.** The **High Warden** and the **Concord of Gates**; ranks from Gatewatch →
  Warden → Gatekeeper → Marshal → High Warden, each a real threshold of trust and access.
- **Territory & resources.** The Wardenmarch (Emberreach), Highgate (the Sundered Sky), and a Warden
  hold at every Gate and every Coil threshold — the most distributed power in the world, and the
  keeper of the way up the Spiral.
- **Military & economy.** Disciplined, elite, few; funded by the Crowns and by the toll of the Gates.
- **Allies / enemies.** Uneasy ally of the Kindled Crowns; opposed by the Free Forges and the
  Gate-Breakers; hunts the Cinder Circle.
- **Internal fault-line.** The oath to *guard* the craft is becoming the thing that *kills* it — a
  growing faction of young Wardens argue the Gates must open faster or the knowledge dies locked.
- **Recruitment & rewards.** Earned through tested loyalty and gate-service; grants rank-gated access
  to sealed Coils, Warden Forgework, and the only sanctioned path up the Great Spiral.
- **(Engine reading: the Wardens ARE the rank system and the quality gates. Their whole politics is
  `authorization before capability`.)**

### The Free Forges — *Free the craft* (creation)
- **History.** Guilds and city-republics that arose in the Rekindling on one conviction: gate-keeping
  is how knowledge dies, and the craft belongs to everyone who can learn it.
- **Philosophy.** *A locked craft is a dead craft.* The Unforging was a tragedy, not a verdict; the
  answer to a broken world is more makers, not fewer.
- **Leadership & hierarchy.** Deliberately flat — a rotating **Open Council** of guild-speakers, no
  crown, merit and works over rank. Chaotic, generative, quarrelsome.
- **Territory & resources.** The city-republics of Emberreach and the Kollforge's radical Vent-Forges;
  the fastest-innovating, least-governable power.
- **Military & economy.** Weak central force, immense economic and craft output; wins by out-making,
  not out-fighting.
- **Allies / enemies.** Allied to the Salvage Guilds and the Emberwright Rememberers; opposed to the
  Warden Concord; wary of their own radical wing, the Gate-Breakers.
- **Internal fault-line.** *How free is free?* The Gate-Breakers want every Gate opened *now*,
  consequences be damned; the moderates fear a second Unforging done in the name of freedom.
- **Recruitment & rewards.** Open to any Forger who makes something worth the Council's notice; grants
  the widest access to profession masteries, open Forgework, and unsanctioned knowledge.

### The Kindled Crowns — *Govern the craft* (order)
- **History.** The feudal kingdoms that survived the Long Cinder by treating Forging as **statecraft** —
  a managed resource for armies, walls, and roads.
- **Philosophy.** *The craft is a river; a kingdom builds the banks.* Not to guard it or free it, but
  to *use* it, stably, for the many.
- **Leadership & hierarchy.** Crowns and courts; classic feudal ladder; the state religion is the
  Church of the First Flame.
- **Territory & resources.** The great relit capitals — Emberreach City and its peers on every Reach;
  the roads, the walls, the standing armies, the largest population.
- **Military & economy.** The strongest conventional power and the deepest treasury; the world's
  stabilizing weight.
- **Allies / enemies.** Uneasy ally of the Wardens; rivalrous with the Free Forges; patron and target
  of a dozen court intrigues.
- **Internal fault-line.** *Whose hand is on the river?* Succession, rival crowns, and courtiers who
  would seize the craft as a private weapon keep the Crowns at war with themselves.
- **Recruitment & rewards.** Service, birth, or usefulness to a court; grants title, land, standing
  armies at your back, and the political questlines that decide who rules a Reach.

## THE ORDER FACTIONS (the player's mechanical home — the Forge-Orders)

Guilds are the institutional spine of professions and the in-world face of the engine's rank ladder;
each is a hall (a reusable room-cluster) that teaches, ranks, and equips. Rising in an Order gates
content and grants its craft.

- **The Warcraft Orders** (combat jobs) — the Vanguard, the Pathfinder, the martial Coilwrights; the
  path of the blade, the bow, and the held line.
- **The Making Orders** (crafting) — Smithing, Enchanting, Inscription, Alchemy, and **Forging proper**
  (the meta-craft, the rarest hall).
- **The Gathering Orders** — Emberreaping, Mining, Foraging, Salvage; the supply of the world.
- **The Knowing Orders** — the **Chroniclers** (lore + the Great Archive), the Wardens (administration
  + gates), the **Cartographers** (exploration + the map). *(The Chroniclers keep the Great Chronicle:
  to be recorded is to persist.)*

Order standing is orthogonal to the great-power argument — a Sworn Chronicler may serve a Crown, a
Concord, or no one, and the tension between an Order's craft-loyalty and a power's politics is a rich
source of quests.

## THE CAUSE FACTIONS (allyable, story-bearing)

- **The Emberwright Rememberers** — scholars hunting the lost First-Forger craft; the **main-story
  faction**. Idealists convinced the golden age can be recovered; their questline is the spine of the
  campaign, and their great fear is that recovering it recovers whatever broke it.
- **The Cinder Circle** — students of the Unforged and the entropy-craft; feared, hunted by the
  Wardens, and *sometimes necessary* — the only ones who understand the Cinderdeep and how to contain
  a live Unforging. Their heresy: unmaking is a craft too, and a world that cannot unmake cannot renew.
- **The Salvage Guilds** — recover Forgework from ruins; the **crafting/economy faction**. Pragmatic,
  wealthy, ethically flexible; they open the drowned cities and the Scoured Ruins the scholars only
  theorize about.
- **The Gate-Breakers** — radicals who want every Gate opened *now*. The Free Forges' dangerous edge;
  heroes to some, the next Unforging waiting to happen to others.
- **The Silent Anvil** — a hidden order that believes some things should stay Unforged; the **conscience
  of the world**. They worship the *potential* in unshaped Ember and hold that the greatest act is
  restraint. They are the faction that asks, in every crisis, the setting's ultimate question: *should
  this power exist at all?*

## RELIGIONS AS FACTIONS

Faith in Aethryn is faith about **making**, and the churches are political powers in their own right.

- **The Church of the First Flame** (orthodox) — reveres the Forge as creator; to Forge is to worship,
  to Unforge is the one sin. State religion of the Kindled Crowns; temples grant blessings (buffs),
  pilgrimages (quests), and holy days (world events).
- **The Cinder Mysteries** (heterodox) — revere the loop: unmaking is holy too, for without Cinder
  there is no new Ember. Persecuted, resilient, entangled with the Cinder Circle.
- **The Silent Anvil** (mystic) — the potential in unshaped Ember is the holy thing; restraint is the
  greatest work. (Both a religion and a cause faction — its two faces reinforce each other.)
- **Ancestor-Forging** (folk) — every culture keeps its dead in Forgescript, believing a remembered
  maker is never fully unmade. The most widespread and least political faith, and the one that ties
  the player's own works into the Great Chronicle.

## Design principle

Every faction is *right about something* and *dangerous about something else*, and the player is never
handed a correct side. The reputation system, the standing conflicts, and the world-events keep
re-litigating the same argument — guard, free, or govern the craft — so the political engine runs for
a decade without resolving. New Reaches ship new local expressions of these powers (a Rimefall Silent
Anvil, a Kollforge Gate-Breaker cell, a Quenchmere Salvage-Guild fleet), so the argument travels the
whole world and never repeats itself exactly.
