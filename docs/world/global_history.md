# Global History — the Deep Timeline of Aethryn

*History players discover, not create. Every era below is buried in the world as ruins, journals,
songs, and scars — never handed over as exposition. Dates are reckoned in **Rekindling Years (RY)**,
counting forward from the opening of the first Gate; everything before is measured backward as
**Before the Cinder (BC)** and, deeper still, **Anvil-Age (AA)**, a time the world only half-remembers.*

This expands the six eras of [`../world_bible.md`](../world_bible.md) §2 into the campaign's spine.
Each age is an expansion tier: the closer to the Forge and the First Seed, the higher the level and
the greater the danger of learning what you find.

---

## The Kindling (AA, the unmeasured dawn)

Before there were years there was only the **Forge** and its overflow. **Ember** poured out and
cooled, and the first shapes that held were not made by anyone: the **Anvil** (the world's forged
crust), the **Forgeward Road** (the frontier running east to the source, its marches deepening toward
the Forge), and the **cooling-seas** that quenched the edges. This is the only age with no makers in
it, and the only one no ruin survives from, because nothing here was *built*, it merely *set*. Forgers
who travel far enough east along the Road find the Kindling's residue: rooms of raw uncooled shape
where the laws of the world are still soft. **(Raid tier. The world's origin, reachable only at the far end.)**

## The Age of the Emberwrights (AA → the First Forgers)

The first living kinds woke — some Forged deliberately, some *precipitated* out of wild Ember like
crystal out of a solution. Among them rose the **Emberwrights**, the First Forgers: not gods, but the
first who learned that Ember could be *shaped on purpose* and made to hold. They walked the cooling
Anvil and Forged the first cities, the first roads (the **Ember-roads**, still walked today), the
first laws written directly into the substance of a place. The craft was **open** — anyone could
learn — and the world was impossibly generative. This is the golden age Aethryn half-remembers and
wholly mythologizes. Everything an archaeologist digs up from this age is better-made than anything
alive can make now, and that gap is the ache at the center of the setting: **the world was more, once.**

The Emberwrights left no rulers and few names — deliberately, the Rememberers argue, because they
believed a maker should be known by their works, not their titles. What they left instead is
**works**: the great Forgeworks, the deep vaults, the first Seeds.

## The Weaving of the First Seed (the height, and the turn)

The Emberwrights' masterwork was to be their last necessary one: a **self-Forging world** — a
**Seed** that could grow, remake itself, and Forge new life without a maker's hand. It was the
craft's summit: a world that would tend itself so its makers could rest. The **First Seed** was woven
somewhere at the Anvil's heart (its exact place is a central mystery), and for a span the world
sang — self-repairing, self-enriching, alive in a way it has never been since.

Then it broke.

## The Unforging (the Sundering — Year Zero of grief)

The First Seed was **sundered**. What caused it is the world's oldest and least-settled question, and
the setting keeps all three answers live and unproven:

- **Sabotage** — someone, or some faction, broke it on purpose. (Who benefits from a world that
  cannot remake itself? The Wardens have a candidate; the Cinder Circle has another.)
- **Hubris** — the Emberwrights reached past what even they could hold, and the Seed failed under its
  own ambition, the way any over-forged thing shatters.
- **Intent** — the Seed *chose* to come apart, scattering itself rather than be owned or completed;
  the world unmade itself to stay free. (The Silent Anvil half-believes this and half-fears it.)

The Unforging cracked the joined supercontinent along the cooling-seas into the seven **Reaches**;
tore whole lands loose to hang unfallen in the sky; and dropped others out of the world entirely into
the **Cinderdeep** below, where shapes never re-cooled. High Forging shattered — not lost as a book is
lost, but *scattered*, its knowledge broken into fragments strewn across every Reach and every age.
The Emberwrights vanished: dead, hidden, or ascended into the Forge — no account agrees. Reality
itself became **fragile**: a thing badly-enough Unforged near you can still, rarely, come apart at a
touch. **(Recurring world-event. The Unforging is not a past tense; small Unforgings still happen,
and stopping one is an endgame crisis.)**

## The Long Cinder (BC, the dark age — roughly a thousand years)

In the grief and terror after the Sundering, Forging became the thing that broke the world, and so it
became **forbidden**. The **Wardens** arose — not conquerors but survivors who took an oath: to guard
the surviving **Gates** (the thresholds where the craft could still be reached) and the surviving
**Seeds** (the few unbroken world-fragments) so that no half-taught Forger could ever Unforge the
world again. They built the **Wardenmarch**, wrote the craft behind ranks and rites, and turned
knowledge into something you had to be *trusted* with. Most of the old craft was lost in this age —
not destroyed, but *locked*, and a lock kept long enough is as good as a grave.

The Long Cinder is the age of ruins-become-tombs, of villages that forgot they were once Forge-cities,
of the Ashborn learning to salvage rather than make. It is also the age that shaped the world's
central political argument, still unresolved: **the Wardens saved the world by locking the craft, and
by locking it may be letting it die.**

## The Age of Rekindling (RY, now — the play era)

Something changed. The **Gates began to open** — slowly, unevenly, some say because the scattered
Ember is drifting back toward coherence, some say because the First Seed's fragments are trying to
find each other. Ember flows freely enough now that *ordinary adventurers* stumble into fragments of
the old craft and, against a thousand years of law, begin to Forge again. This is where the players
stand: at the reopening of a craft that once broke the world, in a race between those who would
**guard** it (the Warden Concord), **free** it (the Free Forges), and **govern** it (the Kindled
Crowns).

The Rekindling is not a triumph. It is a wager. Every player who learns to Forge is a small,
dangerous experiment in whether a broken world should be given the power to remake itself. The main
story is the slow gathering of the scattered craft, the run east along the Forgeward Road toward the
answer, and the recurring, terrible question of the endgame: having relearned how to make a world,
*should we make the First Seed again?*

- **RY 0** — the first Gate opens in the Kindlands; the Rekindling is dated from it.
- **RY 1–now** — the play era. Guilds re-form, capitals relight their forges, Wardens tighten and
  Free Forges push, the Cinderdeep stirs, and small Unforgings begin, for the first time in an age, to
  happen again.

## The through-line, as a player lives it

An adventurer wakes on a cold shore with no memory of the craft and one guttering ember (the flagship
seed's opening). They learn, they travel, and the deeper they go, down the Cinderdeep, east along the
Forgeward Road, back through the ruins of the Long Cinder toward the Emberwright works and the place
the First Seed broke, the more they are not *conquering* the world but **remembering** it. The endgame is a
homecoming to a golden age that no living thing has seen, and a choice about whether to wake it.

*Design note: no piece of this history is ever spoken as a lecture. It is a burned inscription, a
salvager's half-true song, two rival factions telling you opposite versions of the Unforging, a
journal that ends mid-sentence, a Warden's oath, a room where the laws are visibly soft. The player
assembles the timeline the way an archaeologist does — and is never sure they have it all.*
