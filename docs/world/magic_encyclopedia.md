# Magic Encyclopedia — the Schools of Forging

*There is no "magic" in Aethryn as a thing apart from the world. There is only **Forging** — the
shaping of **Ember** — and what other worlds call spells are its lesser, faster forms: shapes a Forger
holds for a moment instead of building to last. Magic here is ancient, dangerous, mysterious, and
woven into every civilization, because every civilization is *made of* the same substance the Forger
bends. This encyclopedia lays out the disciplines, their dangers, and the legendary works of the
craft.*

**The first principle.** A true Forging *stays* — a blade, a bridge, a law, a room. A **Casting** is a
Forging that does *not* stay: Ember shaped and released, the shape fading as the Ember cools back to
possibility. Cheaper, faster, weaker, and reversible — the craft's everyday hand. Most Jobs cast; only
masters *build*. The endgame of the craft is to make a shape so true it never cools at all — which is
what the First Seed was, and why making one again is the setting's ultimate temptation and terror.

**The five elements of the craft** — the natures Ember can be bent toward — are **flame, frost,
lightning/gale, stone/earth, and the lightless triad (shadow, venom, curse)**, with the two
consecrated forms, **radiance** (the Church's holy fire) and the raw **Forgelight** at the source.
Every Casting carries a nature, and every made thing resists some and fears others (the engine's
elemental system, read as cosmology).

## The Disciplines (schools of the craft)

Each discipline is a *way of shaping*, taught by an Order, deepened by mastery, and dangerous in its
own way. A Forger may learn several; few master more than one.

### Combat-shaping — *the fast, released forms*
The everyday craft of the Warcraft Orders: Casting Ember into a blow. **Strikes** carry an element
(freeze the fire-thing, don't burn it); **brands** lay a lingering nature on a foe (a burn that saps,
a chill that slows); **control** shapes bind rather than break (a **daze** that stills a foe, a
**weaken** that blunts its blows). Combat-shaping is the first magic a Forger learns and the last they
perfect — its whole art is choosing the right nature and the right shape for the thing in front of you.

### Support & Doctrine-shaping — *shapes laid on an ally or the self*
Wards, edges, and blessings — a barrier that turns half a blow, an analysis that reveals a foe's weak
point, an order's doctrine that hardens or sharpens those sworn to it. The quiet craft that decides
whether a party lives; the domain of the Making and Knowing Orders and the Church.

### Mending — *the craft turned inward*
Healing is Forging a body back toward the shape it should hold. It is *hard*, because a living thing
resists being remade even toward health, and it is holy, because it is the craft used only to restore.
The Church of the First Flame calls it the truest Forging; the Cinder Mysteries distrust it as a
refusal of the loop.

### Waymaking — *the craft of distance* → see also the Transportation Guide (folded in the Build Order)
Ember bent to move a body across space: the **Ember-roads** (permanent Forgework, the world's
highways), **Gates** (Warden-kept thresholds between distant Forgeworks), **way-shrines** and
**mage-portals** (personal, costly, restricted), and the lost art of true translation the Emberwrights
walked and no one living can. Waymaking is the most guarded craft, because a Forger who can move
freely can reach what the Wardens lock.

### Making-shaping — *the craft in a workshop* → see Crafting (`../world_bible.md` §17-18)
Smithing, Enchanting, Inscription, Alchemy: Forging that *stays*, worked slowly at a station into gear,
runes, potions, and wards. The bridge between magic and the economy; the Making Orders' whole life.

### Farspeech & Chroniclecraft — *the craft of word and memory*
Forging Ember into signal and record: farspeech between distant Forgers, and the **Chroniclecraft**
that writes a thing into the **Great Chronicle** so it *persists* — the craft of the Knowing Orders,
and the metaphysical root of Ancestor-Forging (to be recorded is to be un-unmakeable). *(Engine
reading: the Chronicle is the ledger; to be recorded is to persist.)*

### Reading & Divination — *the craft of seeing what is*
Investigation-shaping: reading the history burned into a place, the nature of a foe (**examine**), the
true name of a made thing, the seams where the world was Forged and where it was Unforged. The
Cartographers' and Chroniclers' art, and the player's tool for the world's mysteries.

### Warding & Protection — *the craft of the held line*
Barriers, sanctuaries, and the great **Gate-wards** that keep the Cinderdeep's mouths shut and the
Spiral's thresholds sealed. The Wardens' foundational discipline: not to make, but to *keep*.

### Summoning & Binding — *the craft of calling a shape*
Calling a Forged servant or a bound spirit into a held shape — the Kollforge's Vent-Forges and the
Deeprooted's grove-callers work it hardest. Dangerous: a bound thing wants to be free, and an unbound
summon is a small Unforging.

### Environmental Forging — *the craft on the world itself*
The largest common magic: reshaping a place — mending a broken bridge, opening a sealed way, calming a
live Unforging, turning a room's law. The visible proof that in Aethryn the world is data a master can
edit. *(Engine reading: quest effects that open doors, reforge bridges, change world-state.)*

## The Forbidden and the Lost

- **Unforging (forbidden).** The craft turned to *unmaking* — deliberately reducing a made thing back
  to Cinder. The one sin of the Church; the study of the Cinder Circle; the thing that broke the
  world. Sometimes the only way to stop a worse Unforging, which is why it can never be fully outlawed
  and never be trusted.
- **Seed-weaving (lost).** The Emberwrights' summit craft: Forging a *self-Forging* thing, a shape that
  makes new shapes on its own. The First Seed was its masterwork. Recovering even a fragment is the
  main-story prize — and the thing every faction fears in the others' hands.
- **True Translation (lost).** The Emberwrights' waymaking, walking between Forgeworks without a Gate.
  Fragments survive as way-shrines; the whole art is gone with its makers.
- **Ancient Rituals.** Multi-Forger works too great for one hand — relighting a dead capital's forge,
  sealing a Reach's Unforging, opening a sealed Coil. The domain of raids and world-events: magic that
  takes a guild, not a hero.

## The Legendary Item Catalog (relic-powered craft)

The greatest works of the craft are not spells but **things** — Forgework so true it never cooled,
carrying its maker's intent across the ages. A legendary relic is always a *question* as much as a
reward: who made it, what did they mean it for, and should you use it as they intended?

- **The Emberwright Regalia** — surviving works of the First Forgers: a hammer that mends what it
  strikes, a lens that reads a thing's true name, a compass that points to the nearest fragment of the
  First Seed. Better-made than anything alive can make; each is a questline and a temptation.
- **The Warden Keys** — the master-works that open the sealed Gates and Coils; the Concord's most
  guarded relics, and the Gate-Breakers' most coveted prize.
- **The Cinder Relics** — things drawn *whole* from the Unforging: they carry the craft of unmaking,
  and they are as dangerous to hold as to face. The Cinder Circle's dark library.
- **The Reach-Relics** — each Reach's signature legendary: the Rimefall's unmelting **Stillheart**, the
  Kollforge's **Vent-Crown**, the Quenchmere's **Drowned Pilot's compass** that points true through any
  dark, the Verdance's living **Boughward**, the Sundered Sky's **Anchor-Stone** that never falls.
- **The Seed-Shards** — the scattered fragments of the First Seed itself: the ultimate relics, the goal
  of the main story, and the pieces that, gathered, could reforge the world — or Unforge it again. The
  campaign's endgame is a hunt for these and a choice about what to do when they are whole.

*Design principle: a legendary is never just bigger numbers. It is a piece of the world's history you
now carry, with a story that continues after you find it — a relic the factions want, the Chronicle
remembers, and the world reacts to. Rewarding curiosity means the best things are found by the curious,
not sold by a vendor.*
